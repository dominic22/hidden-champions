# Related use cases

Ideas for other curated datasets in the same spirit as hidden champions: topics people care about, but that aren’t as easy as looking up a single Forbes-style list. The value is stitching people, assets, organizations, and influence from scattered public sources.

## Pattern (what “hidden champions–shaped” means)

Good signals:

- No single canonical roster
- Value is in joins (people ↔ assets ↔ orgs)
- Public-but-scattered sources
- Network / graph is the payoff

Weak signals:

- One Wikipedia or Forbes page is enough
- Pure rankings
- Purely private / unverifiable data
- Flat directory with no relationships

---

## Strong fits

### Shadow influencers (power without the title)

People who shape markets and policy without being elected or a household-name CEO: board chairs, family-office principals, lobbyists, think-tank funders, “kingmakers.”

- Roster is fuzzy on purpose
- Enrichment: donations, board seats, co-investments, media ownership
- Closest sibling to this repo’s politics/media layer

### Dynastic wealth (the family, not the face)

Track clans (e.g. Wertheimer, Walton, Albrecht, Ambani) as the unit of analysis.

- Who controls what
- Succession and marriages between dynasties
- Holding companies and citizenship splits

Forbes lists individuals; the structure is the story. Reuses the most of the current person/org/asset schema.

### Corporate control chains

Who really owns or controls major companies through layers of trusts, foundations, and shell entities.

- Sources: SEC filings, corporate registries, leak data (Panama/Pandora, etc.)
- High curiosity; low “one list” answers

### Private capital operators

PE/VC partners and family offices that own large chunks of the real economy but aren’t always “billionaires” on a public list.

- Portfolio companies
- LP networks and club deals
- Political ties

### Media & narrative control

Not “top newspapers,” but who funds or owns the outlets, podcasts, newsletters, and think tanks that set frames.

- Cross-link to donations and board seats
- Public narrative vs. actual ownership

---

## Slightly different, still high interest

### Ultra-private assets as the primary object

Start from the asset (superyachts, private jets, islands, sports teams), map back to beneficial owners.

- People love the objects; ownership is the hard part
- Inverts the current “person → assets” direction

### Philanthropy as soft power

Foundations as influence machines.

- Where money goes and which causes
- Which politicians / universities get access
- Sources: OpenSecrets, Form 990, foundation reports

### Cross-border tax geography

Citizenship, residence, HQ vs. tax domicile vs. asset location — more map product than person list.

- Complements the existing “geography & power” enrichment layer
